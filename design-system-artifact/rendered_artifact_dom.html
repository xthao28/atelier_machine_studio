ATELIER MACHINE is an independent art and technology studio that makes curious machines, interactive objects and artificial lifeforms. Its tagline is Making Machines Feel Alive. Technology is a medium for artistic expression, not just a tool. Everything built from this system should read as a retro-futurist publication from a small studio that makes things with its hands: art direction first, engineering craft second, never a tech-news feed, a gaming brand or a corporate AI company.

Content fundamentals

  • Voice. Curious, thoughtful, plainspoken: a maker explaining something they are excited about. Say what the machine does, then what it feels like. "A small physical creature that senses light, learns from every encounter, and slowly becomes someone."
  • Person and tense. "We" for the studio, "you" only to invite a swipe or a follow. Present tense for what a machine does, past tense for build steps.
  • Casing. Sentence case for headlines and body. UPPER-CASE only in IBM Plex Mono labels (label, meta), tags and the wordmark. The tagline is always Making Machines Feel Alive. with initial capitals and a full stop.
  • Length. Headlines at most 8 words; one idea per slide; body text at most 25 words on a slide. A question is a fine headline: "What is Physical AI?"
  • Honest specifics. Name real parts and values (ESP32, LDR + 10 kΩ, 3.3 V). No hype words ("revolutionary", "next-gen"), no emoji, no exclamation marks.
  • Languages. English first; Vietnamese in the same styles. Space Grotesk and IBM Plex Mono both carry Vietnamese diacritics, so keep Làm cho máy móc như có sự sống. at the same size as its English line.
  • Naming. XUNA is always upper-case. Projects are numbered (PRJ—001), experiments are EXP—014, build logs are LOG—007.

Visual foundations

  • Direction. Contemporary retro-futurism: 1970s space-age graphics, 1980s–90s computing, vintage industrial design and early digital interfaces, art-directed rather than decorated. Retro and futuristic at the same time.
  • Colour. Ground is paper (45%) or ink (30%). Accents are lime (technical), lilac (concept) and orange (artificial life, XUNA), roughly 10 / 10 / 5%. Text is ink on light fields and paper on ink; secondary text is muted. Do not use all five colours in every post, and do not introduce another dominant colour. Build a light and a dark version of every layout by swapping surface and on-surface.
  • Type. Space Grotesk for display, headlines and running text (display-xl to caption); IBM Plex Mono for IDs, labels, timestamps, coordinates and annotations (label, meta, data). Display type is tight (−.03 to −.045em), mono labels are open (+.08em). Never set a paragraph in mono. Minimums on a 1080 px canvas: body 28, caption 24, label 22, metadata 20.
  • Layout. Modular grid, asymmetric but balanced. Margin margin-post (72 px), 6 columns with a gutter of 24 px, bands for header (y 72) and footer (y 1222). One dominant element per slide, generous negative space, and one deliberate break of the grid.
  • Borders. Outlines in rule at stroke-line (3 px); dividers at stroke-hair (2 px); the pixel frame at stroke-heavy. Corners are square (radius-none); only tags are pills (radius-pill) and pins, step numbers and status discs are round (radius-round).
  • Depth. Flat. No shadows, no gradients, no glow. Depth comes from the halftone fade, paper grain and overlapping blocks.
  • Texture. Paper grain on light slides (grain-opacity), halftone on image frames and large panels (halftone-opacity, halftone-pitch), dot grid for diagrams (dot-pitch), technical grid at grid-pitch. Textures are quiet and never sit under body text.
  • Imagery. Real hardware photography (bench, breadboard, printed parts), pixel art, and technical diagrams drawn from this system's graphic language. No stock, no generated robot imagery, no 3D renders of XUNA.
  • Motion (video covers). Cuts and 8-frame pixel steps, never easing blurs or glitch effects.
  • Avoid. Generic cyberpunk, neon, heavy gradients, glitch, sci-fi HUDs, overly dark compositions, gaming graphics, excessive pixelation, clutter and unexplained decoration.

Iconography and logo

  • There is no logo mark yet. Set the name ATELIER MACHINE as the wordmark: Space Grotesk 700, upper-case, +.14em. Do not draw or invent a mark; add the real one to assets/ when it exists.
  • There is no icon set. Use the twelve geometric symbols and the numbered pins from Graphic Language; each has one fixed meaning. No emoji, no glyph icons.

XUNA

  • XUNA is an existing orange pixel-art character. Use the supplied reference art at exact pixel multiples with nearest-neighbour scaling inside CharacterFrame. Never redraw, recolour, smooth or re-proportion it, and never turn it into a robot, animal or 3D character.
  • Until the reference art is supplied, every XUNA slot keeps the labelled placeholder (XUNA reference art pending). Do not invent a canonical design.

Using this system

  • Start from a template family (TemplateProjectReveal, TemplateBuildLog, TemplateTechExplainer), assemble slides from the components, and keep the header and footer on every slide.
  • Formats, grids and safe areas are in Social formats; graphic elements in Graphic language; the six template families in Templates; do and don't rules in Usage guidelines.
  • Sample copy in the previews is illustrative. Replace it with the studio's real names, parts, numbers and photographs.

Social formats

Design every post on a 1080 px wide canvas. All sizes below are canvas pixels.

Instagram carousel · 4:5 · 1080 × 1350

  • Use for project introductions, explainers, hardware breakdowns and development updates.
  • Margin margin-post (72 px) on all sides; inner area 936 × 1206. 6 columns, column 136 px, gutter 24 px.
  • Header band y 72–124 (BrandHeader), footer band y 1222–1278 (BrandFooter). Content lives between y 150 and y 1190.
  • The profile grid crops 4:5 to 3:4: keep titles and key marks at least 34 px from the left and right edges.
  • Slide 1 is the cover and carries the title; slides 2 to n−1 carry one idea each; the last slide is the call to action.

Instagram Reels and TikTok · 9:16 · 1080 × 1920

  • Use for video covers, project demonstrations, build process, experiment videos and short-form stories.
  • Conservative safe box: x 72–920, y 250–1470 (safe-top-vertical 250, safe-bottom-vertical 450, safe-right-vertical 160). 4 columns, gutter 24 px.
  • Keep every word and key visual inside the safe box. Backgrounds, textures and blocks may bleed to the edges.
  • Covers are cropped to 3:4 on profile grids: keep the title between y 240 and y 1680, preferably inside the safe box.
  • Header sits at y 270, footer at y 1400 inside the safe box. No page numbering on covers.

Square · 1:1 · 1080 × 1080

  • Use for announcements, profile-related graphics and compact assets.
  • Margin 72 px; 6 columns as 4:5. Header y 72, footer y 952.
  • One idea per square: a title and one image, or a quote.

Spacing scale

  • space-1 8 · space-2 16 · space-3 24 · space-4 32 · space-5 48 · space-6 72 · space-7 96. Every gap, padding and offset is one of these values.
  • Align to the 72 px technical grid; text baselines to 8 px.

Alignment

  • Left-align headlines, body text and annotations. Centre only a single short line (a quote attribution, a coordinate under a target).
  • Numbers and mono values align to the left edge of their column; right-align only counters and right-hand context labels in the header and footer.

Graphic language

graphic-language.md

Graphic language

A reusable library of retro-futuristic elements. Each has a defined purpose; if an element has no job on a slide, remove it.

The fifteen elements

  1. Technical grid overlay (Textures, Registration): 72 px module in grid-line; shows the structure of a slide.
  2. Crosshair marker (Registration): marks a corner or a point; four sizes.
  3. Coordinate label (Registration): X 12.40 · Y 08.75 in meta; locates an object.
  4. Circular targeting element (Registration): rings and 30° ticks; isolates one subject.
  5. Retro computer interface frame (Frames): window with title bar for code and screens.
  6. Experiment identification tag (ExperimentId, Labels): EXP—014.
  7. Technical annotation line (TechnicalAnnotation, Annotations): ties a label to a part.
  8. Directional arrow (Annotations): flow, return, emphasis; open head.
  9. Halftone pattern (Textures): fades photos and panels into the paper.
  10. Paper grain (Textures): warm print surface on light slides.
  11. Pixel-art frame (CharacterFrame, Frames): stepped corners for characters.
  12. Industrial information panel (Frames, MetadataBlock): rows of specification.
  13. Geometric symbol (Symbols): twelve glyphs with one meaning each.
  14. Object classification label (Labels): OBJ. CLASS A—3.
  15. Numbered project indicator (ProjectNumber, Labels): 001, 002, 003.

Rules

  • Draw everything in rule colour or one accent fill; never recolour a symbol to match the slide.
  • At most one targeting element, four annotations and two textures per slide.
  • Graphics frame content; they never replace it. Keep body text on a clean area.
  • Draw new elements from the same primitives: 3 px lines, squares, discs, plus marks and 8 px steps.

Usage guidelines

guidelines.md

Usage guidelines

Content hierarchy

  1. The headline or one dominant graphic reads first.
  2. Technical identity (project number, experiment ID, tags) reads second.
  3. Body text and annotations read last. If a slide needs more than 25 words, split it.

Do

  • Use real hardware photographs and the supplied XUNA reference art.
  • Let negative space do the work; leave at least a third of a slide quiet.
  • Pair every status colour with a shape and a word.
  • Keep one accent field per slide and one filled tag.
  • Check each headline in Vietnamese before publishing a bilingual post.

Don't

  • Don't use gradients, glow, shadows, neon or glitch effects.
  • Don't set paragraphs in mono or decorative fonts; don't go below the minimum sizes.
  • Don't place text on halftone, grain-heavy areas or the photo itself without a paper or ink plate.
  • Don't redraw XUNA, don't invent a logo mark, don't add stock or generated robot imagery.
  • Don't add a graphic element without a job from the Graphic language list.

Image treatment

  • Photos: natural colour, shot square-on or at a clear three-quarter angle, on a plain bench. Place them in ImageFrame with the halftone fade at the edge and a mono caption bar.
  • Pixel art: scale by whole multiples, nearest-neighbour, on a flat field with the dot grid at most.
  • Diagrams: lines at 3 px, nodes in lime, lilac, orange, labels in label.

Consistency checklist

  • Header and footer on every slide; same positions on every post.
  • Colours only from the tokens; type only from the styles.
  • Light and dark versions both checked at phone size.

Rules that apply to every family

  • Slide 1 is a cover with one dominant element. Inner slides open with a SectionHeading.
  • Each post has one lead background colour plus at most one dark slide. Do not repeat the same composition on consecutive slides.
  • Every slide carries BrandHeader and BrandFooter; page numbering counts the cover.
  • Light and dark versions swap surface and on-surface; accent fields stay the same colour.
  • Replace placeholders (photo, XUNA) with real material before publishing; never publish a placeholder.
